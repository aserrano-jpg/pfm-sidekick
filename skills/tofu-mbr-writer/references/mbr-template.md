# MBR Template: LAND Section Structure

## Overview

The LAND section of the MBR focuses on customer acquisition and new logo performance. It has two subsections: **Highlights** (performance analysis and why) and **What's Coming** (forward-looking initiatives, fuel the fire, fill the gap). This template ensures consistency across writers (Michael, Armando, Val).

---

## LAND Section Template

### Header
**LAND: [Month/Period] Performance**

---

### Subsection 1: Highlights
Performance analysis with root cause and trend. Why did we perform as we did?

Structure:
1. Lead metric (usually Base Product BD1-6 or acquisition volume)
2. Volume sources breakdown (paid, organic, email, etc. with percentages)
3. Supporting efficiency metrics (CAC, LTV, ROI, margin)
4. Period comparison (month-over-month, vs. target, vs. forecast)
5. Root cause or driver analysis (competitive pressure, product changes, market shift)
6. Forward confidence or projection (if applicable)

**Word count:** 150-250 words

**Example:**
"Base Product BD1-6 reached 0.7 target in May, up from 0.65 in April. Paid channels drove 72% of volume; organic email contributed 28%. CAC rose 8% month-over-month due to increased competition on paid search; however, LTV held steady at 35x CAC, indicating sustainable unit economics. Root cause: three competitors entered the market, each deploying estimated $1.5M monthly spend. Expected CAC trajectory: plateau in June as competitive intensity stabilizes. Confidence: medium, dependent on competitor spend levels. Recommendation: monitor CPC weekly through end of Q2."

---

### Subsection 2: What's Coming
Forward-looking narrative on growth drivers or gap-filling initiatives.

Structure:
1. Planned paid channel initiatives (new audiences, creative, budget shifts)
2. Product or go-to-market changes affecting acquisition
3. Competitive or market context (threats or opportunities)
4. Recommended actions or budget allocation
5. Risks, contingencies, or gates (if applicable)

**Word count:** 150-250 words

**Example:**
"We're launching two new paid audiences in June targeting mid-market; expected volume lift: 15-20%. Creative refresh is pending (delayed one week; Sarah owns; launch: June 3). Allocate $30K June budget to new audiences; hold $50K contingency for competitive bidding pressure. Competitive landscape: three new entrants mean CAC could rise another 10-15% if they match our spend. Risk: if new audiences convert 10% below existing average, scale back to 5% budget allocation. Opportunity: existing customer base offers 5K lookalike volume (untested); recommend 10% test allocation in July. Gate: if CAC hits $85 (current $72), trigger bid reduction on low-funnel terms (trade-off: 8% volume loss, saves $12K/month)."

## Tone Rules (All Writers)

1. **Specific over vague:** Name the metric (CAC, LTV, BD1-6), the channel (paid search, email, organic), the product (Base Product), the team member, the date
2. **Data-led:** Lead with numbers; add narrative context second
3. **No hedging:** Skip "arguably", "interestingly", "it's worth noting"
4. **Action-oriented:** Every subsection should imply or state what to do
5. **No em-dashes:** Use period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead

---

## Word Count Guidelines

- **Highlights subsection:** 150-250 words
- **What's Coming subsection:** 150-250 words
- **Total LAND section:** 300-500 words

---

## Writer-Specific Adjustments

### Michael's Flavor (Highlights)
Emphasizes root cause and confidence level. Adds phrases like "Root cause:", "Expected trajectory:", "Confidence: high/medium/low."

Example: "CAC rose 8%. Root cause: competitive bid escalation from three new market entrants. Expected trajectory: plateau by June. Confidence: medium."

### Armando's Flavor (What's Coming)
Leads with action. Names owner and deadline. Uses phrases like "[Team] owns X by [date]", "Allocate", "Hold contingency", "Trade-off: [what you lose]."

Example: "Launching two new audiences June 3 (Sarah owns creative). Allocate $30K; hold $50K contingency. Trade-off: if new audiences underperform by 10%, reduce allocation to 5%."

### Val's Flavor (Highlights or What's Coming)
Flags second-order effects and scenarios. Uses phrases like "If X, then Y. Confidence: [level]", "Watch for:", "Best/base/worst case:", "Gate: if [metric] hits [threshold], [action]."

Example: "If competitors sustain spend, CAC will rise 15-25% in Q3. Confidence: high. Second-order effect: LTV ratio compresses. Gate: if CAC hits $85, trigger bidding reduction."

---

## Checklist Before Publishing

- [ ] All numbers sourced (no estimates without caveat)
- [ ] All team names and owners spelled correctly
- [ ] Key metrics defined (e.g., BD1-6 target is 0.7)
- [ ] Competitive or risk items named specifically
- [ ] Action items have owner, deadline, and expected outcome
- [ ] Gates or contingencies are threshold-specific (use numbers, not vague ranges)
- [ ] No em-dashes; replace with period, comma, colon, or parentheses
- [ ] Sentence average 12-15 words (short and readable)
- [ ] One metric per sentence where possible
- [ ] Total length 300-500 words (Highlights + What's Coming combined)
